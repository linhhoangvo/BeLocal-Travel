Hoi An, a little closer.

Ideas for seeing more of the town and the people who make it their home. Start with whichever story speaks to you.

COUNTRYSIDE · 4 MIN READ

Beyond the Old Town: Hoi An by bicycle

The Old Town is often where a visit begins. The quieter roads beyond it offer a different sense of place: gardens, riverside homes, workshops and families going about their day. A bicycle gives you time to notice the distance between those moments.

A ride towards Tra Nhieu Village is about more than scenery. Local families continue to make things by hand, including rice paper and woven mats. A guide can introduce the people behind the work and explain how these crafts fit into daily life. If you are invited to try, take your time. The point is to understand the work, rather than rush to the next stop.

Leave space for lunch as well. Sitting down with a family changes the rhythm of the day, and a boat journey along the Thu Bon River makes a calm way back to town.

Before you go: You need to be comfortable riding a bicycle. Wear shoes you can pedal in, bring sun protection and check the pickup area and weather with your guide.

COFFEE & CULTURE · 3 MIN READ

A slower morning in Hoi An

A morning in Hoi An does not have to begin with a checklist. Choose a street you have not walked before, find a place to sit and let the first cup of coffee set the pace.

Look beyond the menu for a moment. Some cafés are busy meeting places; others are small, quiet corners where you can hear the town waking up. A conversation about a drink can lead to a conversation about the neighbourhood, the building or how the owner spends the day.

Try leaving the next hour open. Walk a little between stops, pause when something catches your eye and follow your curiosity. This is a simple way to enjoy Hoi An when the busiest streets start to feel crowded.

Before you go: Ask what drinks are included in any guided coffee experience, and check the meeting point and duration before booking.

FOOD & DRINK · 3 MIN READ

When Hoi An comes out after dark

Lantern light changes the feel of Hoi An, but the evening is not limited to its best-known streets. A walk with a local guide can connect the food, drinks and small venues you might otherwise pass by.

The pleasure is in the changes of pace: a cocktail in a tucked-away bar, something savoury at a food stop, a sweet pause at a café and one last drink before the night ends. Moving on foot lets each stop feel like part of the town, rather than a separate destination.

You do not need to know the local bar scene before arriving. Tell your guide what you like to eat and drink, and ask about vegetarian food or non-alcoholic options if you prefer them. Exact venues and menus can change, so leave a little room for surprise.

Before you go: The cocktail experience is for adults aged 18 and over. Check the start time, meeting point and what is included on the booking page.

ART & TRADITION · 3 MIN READ

Make room for calligraphy and tea

Not every memorable hour needs a long route. Sometimes it begins at a table, with a brush in your hand and someone showing you how to slow down.

A calligraphy session offers a closer look at the attention behind each mark. Watching a teacher work is one thing; trying it yourself gives you a different appreciation for the control, patience and practice involved. Tea brings a natural pause to the experience and makes room for conversation.

Come without worrying about making a perfect piece. Ask questions, notice how the tools are used and enjoy the process. It can be a welcome change of pace between busier days of sightseeing.

Before you go: Confirm the session length, location, materials and any take-home work before reserving.
